HACCP & Food Safety Management

What is HACCP?

Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) is a systematic method for identifying, evaluating, and controlling food safety hazards, rather than relying on end-inspection alone. UK/EU law requires food businesses to have a documented food safety management system based on HACCP principles.

The 7 principles — overview

  1. Conduct a hazard analysis
  2. Identify Critical Control Points (CCPs)
  3. Establish critical limits
  4. Establish monitoring procedures
  5. Establish corrective actions
  6. Establish verification procedures
  7. Establish record-keeping

Hazard analysis & Critical Control Points

Hazard analysis: map out every step of a food's journey (delivery → storage → prep → cooking → holding → service) and identify where hazards could be introduced.

Critical Control Point (CCP): a step where control is essential to prevent, eliminate, or reduce a hazard to a safe level — e.g. cooking is almost always a CCP, since it's the step that kills harmful bacteria.

Critical limits & monitoring

Critical limit: the measurable value that separates safe from unsafe at a CCP — e.g. "core temperature must reach 75°C."

Monitoring: the planned checks that confirm the critical limit is being met — e.g. probing every batch of chicken and logging the reading.

Corrective action, verification & records

Corrective action: what you do when monitoring shows a critical limit hasn't been met — e.g. return food to cooking until it reaches 75°C, or discard it.

Verification: periodic checks that the whole system is working as intended (e.g. management spot-checks, calibrating equipment).

Records: written evidence — temperature logs, cleaning schedules, delivery checks — that prove due diligence to an EHO.

Safer Food, Better Business (SFBB)

Most small and medium food businesses in the UK don't need to write a HACCP plan completely from scratch — the FSA's free Safer Food, Better Business pack provides a ready-made, simplified food safety management system built on HACCP principles, with diary sheets for daily checks.

Summary

  • HACCP is a proactive, step-by-step system for controlling food safety hazards.
  • A Critical Control Point is a step where control is essential — cooking is the classic example.
  • Records prove your system is actually being followed, day to day.
