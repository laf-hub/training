Module 9 of 10

Kitchen-Specific Practice

Everything so far applies wherever food is handled. This module focuses on practices specific to back-of-house kitchen work.

Duration: 5–6 minutes

What this module covers

  • Goods-in checks
  • Separating raw and ready-to-eat food
  • Probing every batch, not just the first item
  • Storage, stock rotation, and waste management

Goods-in checks

Kitchen Check every delivery: temperature (chilled ≤8°C, frozen ≤-18°C on arrival), use-by dates, packaging integrity, and signs of pest activity or damage. Reject and record anything that fails — don't put it into storage "to sort out later."

Raw vs ready-to-eat separation

Kitchen Use separate prep areas, boards, and utensils for raw and ready-to-eat food wherever the kitchen layout allows. Where space is tight, always prep ready-to-eat food first, before raw meat/fish touches the same surface, and fully clean and disinfect between the two.

Probing in a working kitchen

Kitchen Probe every batch of high-risk food, not just "the first one out." Keep probe wipes at every station where probing happens, and log checks at busy service points (e.g. Sunday roast, batch-cooked curries) — these are exactly the situations where corners get cut under pressure.

Storage & stock rotation

Kitchen Follow FIFO (First In, First Out): newer stock goes behind or under older stock so older items get used first. Store raw meat/fish on the lowest shelves of the fridge, in sealed containers, below ready-to-eat and cooked food.

Kitchen waste management

Kitchen Use foot-operated, lidded bins in prep areas. Separate food waste for collection where required. Empty bins regularly during service, not just at close — overflowing bins attract pests and are a physical/chemical contamination risk.

Summary

  • Check every delivery before it goes into storage.
  • Separate raw and ready-to-eat food by space and time.
  • Probe every batch, not just the first item.
  • FIFO stock rotation and prompt waste removal reduce risk.
Quick self-check (click to reveal answer)

Q: Where in the fridge should raw meat be stored relative to ready-to-eat food?

A: On the lowest shelf, below ready-to-eat food, in a sealed container.
