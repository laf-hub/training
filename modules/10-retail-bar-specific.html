Retail & Bar-Specific Practice

This final content module covers practices specific to front-of-house retail counters and bar service, including cheese and deli counter practice.

Duration: 16–20 minutes

What this module covers

  • Receiving chilled cheese, and what to do with a damaged or out-of-temperature delivery
  • Cheese cutting: knives, boards, and wrapping
  • Slicers on a deli counter
  • Wooden shelving and display counters
  • Date labelling and stock rotation
  • Cellar and cold storage hygiene
  • Cleaning food-contact equipment
  • Glass handling and breakages
  • Allergen communication and traceability at the counter
  • Hand hygiene at the till

Receiving chilled cheese

Retail Treat a cheese delivery with the same rigour as any other high-risk chilled product (Module 5 and Module 7's worked example): probe the temperature, don't just trust the van's readout, check it's at or below the agreed chilled limit. Check packaging is intact, with no torn film, blown vacuum packs, or crushed wheels. Check the batch code and use-by date against the delivery note, and log all of it before the cheese goes into storage.

Damaged or out-of-temperature deliveries

Retail If a delivery arrives damaged (crushed packaging, a leaking rind, a torn vacuum pack) or out of temperature, don't put it into stock while you decide what to do. Set it aside, check it properly (more than one probe reading, a proper look at the packaging), and if you're unsure whether it's acceptable, ask a supervisor rather than guessing. Reject it if it fails, and record the delivery, the fault, and what you did about it, the same discipline as any other goods-in check.

Cheese cutting: knives and boards

Retail Use a clean knife and board for each cheese, or clean thoroughly between different cheeses, especially between a plain cheese and one coated or flavoured with a major allergen (nuts, for example). A quick wipe is not the same as the six-stage clean covered in Module 6; residue from a nut-coated rind left on a board or blade can cross-contact the next cheese cut on it.

Keep cheese out of the chilled display or fridge for as short a time as practical while cutting and portioning. A "quick" cutting job that gets interrupted by a queue at the till can quietly turn into cheese sitting at room temperature for far longer than intended.

Cheese cutting: wrapping and labelling

Retail Once cut, wrap or box cheese promptly rather than leaving it exposed on the counter. Label cut portions with the product name, allergen information, and a use-by date appropriate to a cut item, which is often shorter than the whole product's original date, since cutting exposes more surface area to air and handling.

Slicers on a deli counter

Retail A slicer used for one product and then straight onto another without cleaning is exactly the indirect cross-contamination and allergen cross-contact risk covered in Module 2: blade, carriage, and guard can all carry residue forward. Clean and disinfect between different products, not just at the end of the day, and always disconnect power before dismantling any part of it for cleaning. Never override or remove a blade guard to "make it quicker."

Wooden shelving and display counters

Retail Wood looks and feels right for a cheese counter, but it can't be cleaned and disinfected as thoroughly as stainless steel or food-grade plastic: it's porous, and moisture and bacteria can get into the grain in a way a wipe-down won't reach. Use wooden boards and shelving mainly for presentation and display, keep them dry, and don't use them as a direct cutting or prep surface for high-risk food without a proper board on top.

Chilled display counters are a Critical Control Point in their own right (Module 7): check and log the temperature through the day, and don't let stock sit out at the front of an open display for longer than your procedure allows, particularly on a warm day or near a heat source.

Probe thermometers at the counter

Retail Use a probe thermometer to spot-check the display counter itself, not just deliveries, since a display case can run warm at one end or drift over the course of a day without an obvious sign. Clean and disinfect the probe between uses on different products, exactly as covered in Module 5, and log what you find.

Date labelling & stock rotation

Retail / Bar Know the difference: "use by" is a safety date: food must not be sold or used after it (high-risk chilled foods). "Best before" is a quality date: food is still safe after this date, just may not be at its best. Rotate stock using FIFO and check dates during every restock.

Cellar & cold storage hygiene

Bar Cellars should be clean, pest-proofed, and well-ventilated. Clean drip trays and spillages promptly; sugary residue attracts pests and bacteria. Check keg/cask dates and rotate stock. Keep cellar temperature appropriate for the products stored (typically 11–13°C for cask ale).

Cleaning food-contact equipment

Retail / Bar Slicers, knives, boards, and scales that touch food directly need the full clean-then-disinfect process from Module 6, not just a visible-dirt wipe. Beer lines should be professionally cleaned on a set schedule (industry guidance, not a specific legal minimum, commonly recommends every 7 days) to prevent yeast and bacterial build-up that affects both taste and safety. Glass washers need regular descaling and rinse-aid checks; ice machines need regular cleaning too, since ice is a food and can carry contamination just like anything else.

Glass handling & breakages

Bar Have a clear glass-breakage procedure: clear the area immediately, use a dedicated dustpan/brush (not hands), check nearby drinks/ice/food for shards, and dispose of broken glass in a designated sharps-safe container, never in a general food waste bin.

Allergen communication and traceability at the counter

Retail / Bar Have an allergen matrix available at the till/counter for staff to check quickly, and make sure customers know they can ask (Module 8). Remember PPDS labelling rules apply to anything made or cut on-site and displayed for self-selection, like pre-cut cheese portions in a chiller.

Know where to find the batch or traceability code for anything on your counter, on the delivery record if not on the product itself, so a customer question or a supplier recall can be traced back to the exact batch you sold, not just "the cheese we had in that week."

Handling money & food together

Retail / Bar Cash and card machines carry bacteria. Wash or sanitise hands after handling money and before touching food, garnishes, ice, or glass rims; ideally, avoid handling both in the same motion at all.

Summary

  • Check chilled cheese deliveries properly: temperature, packaging, batch code, before they go into stock.
  • Use clean knives and boards between different cheeses, especially around allergen-coated products, and don't let cut cheese sit out too long.
  • Clean slicers between products, not just at the end of the day.
  • Wood is for presentation, not as a direct high-risk prep surface; display counters are a CCP that needs regular temperature checks.
  • Know the difference between "use by" (safety) and "best before" (quality).
  • Keep cellars, lines, ice machines, and all food-contact equipment on a real cleaning schedule.
  • Have a proper glass-breakage procedure.
  • Know your allergen matrix and how to trace a batch, and wash hands between handling money and food.
