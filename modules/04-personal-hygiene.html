Personal Hygiene

Duration: 6–8 minutes

What this module covers

  • Why personal hygiene matters
  • Handwashing: when and how
  • Protective clothing, jewellery, nails and cuts
  • Illness reporting and exclusion periods
  • Rules for food areas

Why it matters

People are one of the biggest sources of food contamination — through unwashed hands, illness, hair, and unclean clothing. Good personal hygiene is one of the simplest and most effective controls you have.

Handwashing: when

  • Before starting work and after breaks.
  • After using the toilet.
  • After handling raw food, waste, or cleaning chemicals.
  • After touching your face, hair, or phone.
  • Before handling ready-to-eat food.
  • After handling money (retail/bar).

Handwashing: how

Wet hands, apply soap, and scrub for at least 20 seconds — palms, backs of hands, between fingers, fingertips, thumbs, and wrists. Rinse thoroughly and dry with a single-use towel or air dryer. Use a dedicated hand-wash basin, not a food-prep sink.

Protective clothing

  • Clean uniform/apron, changed regularly and when visibly dirty.
  • Hair tied back and/or covered.
  • No outdoor clothing worn over food-prep clothing.
  • Remove or cover protective clothing before using the toilet.

Jewellery, nails & cuts

  • Minimal jewellery — typically a plain wedding band only; no rings with stones, bracelets, or dangling earrings near open food.
  • Nails short, clean, and free of nail polish or false nails.
  • Cover cuts and grazes with a blue, metal-detectable plaster, plus a glove where appropriate.

Illness reporting

Report to your supervisor before a shift if you have diarrhoea, vomiting, a fever, or an infected wound. Anyone with diarrhoea or vomiting should not handle food and must stay away from food areas until symptom-free for 48 hours — this is the standard exclusion period used to prevent spreading illnesses like norovirus (Module 3).

This 48-hour rule covers most everyday cases, but it isn't absolute: if a doctor confirms a more serious cause (e.g. E. coli O157 or typhoid), public health authorities can require a longer exclusion and formal clearance before you return to food handling. Always follow what your supervisor and any medical guidance tell you, rather than treating 48 hours as a fixed rule that overrides everything else.

In food areas

No smoking, eating, drinking (other than water from a covered container), or chewing gum in food preparation, storage, or bar-service areas — these all create routes for contamination.

Summary

  • Wash hands thoroughly and at every key point in the day.
  • Keep hair, nails, jewellery, and clothing in check.
  • Report illness before your shift — the 48-hour rule applies to sickness/diarrhoea.

Q: How long should someone stay away from food handling after diarrhoea or vomiting has stopped?

A: 48 hours, symptom-free.
